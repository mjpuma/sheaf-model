\section{Mathematics}
\label{sec:math}

This section writes down the one new layer. Every other equation --- harvest
calendars, hungry-season buffers, AMIS cuts, Armington clearing, asks, the
world-price mix --- is the Gate~0 spine and is not restated. See the Gate~0
white paper, \S3. Quantities are million tonnes per step; prices are real
dollars per tonne (Pink Sheet, MUV 2010).

\subsection{What the layer is doing, in one paragraph}

Each country still wants a baseline pile of food and feed for each grain,
plus (for US maize) an ethanol pile that does not care about wheat or rice
prices. The food-and-feed pile shrinks when that grain is expensive, and
grows a little when a \emph{substitute} grain is expensive. Then each grain
runs the same Gate~0 market it always did: offers, asks, Armington trade,
warehouse. We compute all three demands from this fortnight's three prices,
then all three markets, then the three new prices. That is Jacobi coupling.
Nothing in this paper jointly optimises the three grains, and nothing lets
a government choose a ban.

\subsection{Flex demand with cross prices}
\label{sec:flex}

Gate~0 desired use for grain \(g\) is isoelastic in its own price only,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:own}
d^g_{i,t}
= C^{g,\mathrm{flex}}_{i,t}
  \bigl(p^g_t/p^g_0\bigr)^{\varepsilon_g}
+ C^{g,\mathrm{ind}}_{i,t}.
\end{equation}
Gate~1 keeps that split and multiplies the flex pile by every grain's
relative price:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flex}
d^g_{i,t}
= C^{g,\mathrm{flex}}_{i,t}
  \prod_h \Bigl(\frac{p^h_t}{p^h_0}\Bigr)^{\eta_{gh}}
+ C^{g,\mathrm{ind}}_{i,t}.
\end{equation}
Own exponents are the locked Gate~0 elasticities,
\(\eta_{gg}=\varepsilon_g\) from \texttt{CropParams.elast} (wheat \(-0.15\),
rice \(-0.20\), maize \(-0.25\)). Cross exponents (\(g\neq h\)) are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cross}
\eta_{gh}=\sigma\,\rho_{gh}\,|\varepsilon_g|.
\end{equation}
\(\rho\) is the illustrative substitutability matrix in
\texttt{sheaf/calibration.py}: wheat--maize \(0.40\) (feed), wheat--rice
\(0.30\) (food), rice--maize \(0.20\). It is frozen. \(\sigma\) scales the
whole off-diagonal. Industrial/ethanol does not enter~\eqref{eq:cross}.

At \(p=p_0\) the product is \(1\) for any \(\sigma\), so the calm twin stays
flat. Code evaluates \(\mathtt{fac}=\exp(\eta\log(p/p_0))\) in
\texttt{sheaf/dynamic\_coupled.py}. Grain order is wheat, rice, maize.

\subsection{Jacobi step}
\label{sec:jacobi}

Write \(p_t=(p^{\mathrm{w}}_t,p^{\mathrm{r}}_t,p^{\mathrm{m}}_t)\). One
fortnight is
\begin{enumerate}
\item demand: evaluate~\eqref{eq:flex} at \(p_t\) for all three grains;
\item market: run the Gate~0 offer / Armington / ask / warehouse map on each
      grain separately, with that grain's FAOSTAT network and that grain's
      AMIS diary;
\item prices: each grain's world-price map produces \(p_{t+1}\).
\end{enumerate}
There is no inner loop to a simultaneous multi-grain fixed point, and no
joint quadratic program. Offers and asks remain crop-specific. The only
cross-grain object is the demand product~\eqref{eq:flex}.

\subsection{Dual host (disclose, do not equate)}
\label{sec:dual}

An older annual market-clearing program in \texttt{sheaf/core.py} uses a
linear Slutsky system \(D=a-Mp\) with own-price vector
\texttt{calibration.OWN\_ELAST} (wheat \(-0.25\), rice \(-0.20\), maize
\(-0.30\)) and a constructed positive-definite \(M_i\). That host is not
run here. Switching Gate~1 own-price \(\varepsilon\) to
\texttt{OWN\_ELAST} would break the \(\sigma=0\) identity against Gate~0.
The cross form~\eqref{eq:cross} is a constant-elasticity overlay: the
demand Jacobian is not Slutsky-symmetric. Paper P3 is isoelastic food/feed
on the 24-step spine. It is not README~\S1.

\subsection{What is not in this layer}

Joint multi-grain quadratic program; annual integrable \(M_i\); endogenous
export game; endogenous trade network; convenience yield / futures
smoothing. Those are other papers~\citep{armington1969,takayama1971}.
The paused prototype in \texttt{sheaf/dynamic\_grains.py} is not scored.
