\begin{abstract}
SHEAF is meant, eventually, as a multi-crop model in which governments
choose export bans. Gate~0 locked the single-crop spine: one grain at a
time, a step every two weeks, historical bans from a policy diary, no
switching among grains. This note is Gate~1 (paper P3). It turns on only
the switching. Food and feed respond isoelastically to the other grains'
prices; US maize ethanol does not. Each grain still clears on its own
FAOSTAT network and its own selling prices. A scale \(\sigma\) multiplies
the cross-price exponents. We do not estimate \(\sigma\). We report a
pre-declared band \(\{0,0.3,0.6\}\), declared before looking at coupled
scores. Coupled \(\sigma=0\) recovers Gate~0 world prices to machine
precision on 2006--11 and on 2021--23. In 2007/08, raising \(\sigma\)
raises rice and maize hikes (the substitution sign). Wheat stays
restriction-led in 2008 and production-led in 2010 at every reported
\(\sigma>0\). Isolated rice AMIS still carries essentially all of the 2008
rice hike: substitution is a spillover, not a replacement for
India/Vietnam bans. Agrimate cannot do this layer, because it is
single-commodity~\citep{kuhla2025}. A 2010 maize hike-ratio drop at
\(\sigma=0.6\) is a higher 2009 base after the 2008 wheat--maize
spillover, not a lower 2011 peak. In 2022, wheat stays AMIS-led, maize
stays flat, and rice drifts the wrong way. Gate~0 consumption and shipment
leftovers are not fixed. Governments do not choose bans here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
\label{sec:scope}

SHEAF's destination is a country-level model of wheat, rice, and maize in
which governments choose export restrictions and consumers can switch
among grains when one of them spikes~\citep{kuhla2025,schewe2017}. Gate~0
asked whether each grain's market already works on its own. It did, with
listed leftovers, and those parameters are frozen. This note asks the next
question: when wheat (or rice) spikes, do maize and rice prices move in a
way that isoelastic cross-price demand on that locked market can explain
--- and that three independent single-crop runs cannot?

This note is that claim (Gate~1 / paper P3). It does four things.

\begin{enumerate}
\item State the substitution mathematics without remainder
      (\S\ref{sec:math}), including the dual demand host that is
      \emph{not} this layer.
\item Say, before any coupled score, how \(\sigma\) is identified
      (\S\ref{sec:ident}) and what Pink Sheet 2007/08 actually shows
      (\S\ref{sec:data}).
\item Score the pre-declared band on 2007/08, 2010/11, and the 2022
      Ukraine-war window (\S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:ukraine}),
      with the 2010 maize leftover classified rather than patched
      (\S\ref{sec:e5}).
\item Write down what the band can claim, must disclose, and must not
      claim (\S\ref{sec:claim}).
\end{enumerate}

A supplement maps every new equation to functions in
\texttt{sheaf/dynamic\_coupled.py} (\S\ref{sec:code}). The Gate~0 white
paper remains the spine. An older annual market-clearing program in
\texttt{sheaf/core.py} is a leftover TWIST-era prototype (one tax per
year). It is not the crisis game and it is not run
here~\citep{takayama1971,samuelson1952,headey2011}. Governments that
choose bans do it on this 24-step spine; that is a later paper, and it
does not require re-running Gate~0 or this substitution band.

\textbf{What is switched on.} Food and feed can substitute among wheat,
rice, and maize. The strength of that switching is a reported band, not a
fitted peak.

\textbf{What stays off.} Export cuts are still a historical AMIS diary,
not a choice governments make inside the model. Who trades with whom is
still FAOSTAT. Gate~0 elasticities, warehouse knobs, asks, and the US
maize ethanol leftover are not retuned. We do not add special knobs for
2008 or 2010.

\textbf{What is claimed.} With those restrictions, coupled \(\sigma=0\) is
Gate~0; \(\sigma>0\) spills in the substitute direction in 2007/08 without
flipping wheat's 2008/2010 attribution or replacing rice's own bans. We do
not claim an estimated \(\sigma^\star\), a clean wheat\(\to\)rice test in
2008, or that Gate~0 leftovers went away.

\subsection{A short glossary}

\begin{description}
\item[Gate~0 spine] The locked single-crop fortnightly market: harvest
      shocks, hungry-season buffers, AMIS diary, Armington trade, exporter
      asks. Parameters in the Gate~0 white paper, Table~1.
\item[\(\sigma\)] Scale on cross-price exponents. Zero is three separate
      crops. Larger means stronger switching. Reported as a band, not a
      point.
\item[\(\rho\)] Illustrative substitutability among grains (wheat--maize
      strongest via feed). Frozen in \texttt{calibration.py}.
\item[Jacobi] One step: all three demands from this fortnight's prices,
      then all three markets, then the three new prices.
\item[AMIS] The G20/OECD diary of real export policies. Same map as
      Gate~0.
\item[Pink Sheet] World Bank monthly commodity prices, constant 2010
      dollars (MUV). Same series as Gate~0.
\item[Matched / official split] Harvest shocks plus AMIS, average
      food-and-feed, US maize ethanol on. Same flags as Gate~0.
\item[Dual host] Annual linear Slutsky demand in \texttt{sheaf/core.py}
      versus isoelastic demand on this 24-step spine. Different
      \(\varepsilon\). This paper is the second.
\end{description}

\section{Lineage: what Agrimate cannot do}
\label{sec:lineage}

TWIST~\citep{schewe2017} is annual, single-commodity, no network, no AMIS.
Agrimate~\citep{kuhla2025} adds a 24-step agent-based network with
historical restrictions taken as given. Wheat is the main-text hindcast;
rice and maize are separate single-commodity calibrations in
Supplement~G. If wheat gets expensive, Agrimate's rice consumers do not
notice. That is not a bug in Agrimate. It is the design.

SHEAF exists to put two things on that network that Agrimate does not:
consumers switching among grains, and governments choosing restrictions
rather than following a diary. Gate~0 was the first thing without the
second. Gate~1 is the switching, still without the diary becoming a
choice. Letting governments choose bans is later work on \emph{this}
spine, with types slow and actions \(\tau_t\) responding to
conditions~\citep{headey2011}. It is not this note, and this note does
not need to be re-run to start it.

Food-trade fragility work~\citep{puma2015} motivates looking at who buys
from whom. Export restrictions as a way to keep domestic prices down are
why the AMIS diary exists~\citep{headey2011,martin2012,wright2011}. Cross
grain calorie substitution is a different object: Roberts and
Schlenker~\citep{roberts2013} identify it at a different aggregation. We
use their \emph{qualitative} implication that US maize industrial demand is
a mandate, not an isoelastic consumer, and we keep that block out of
substitution. We do not import their calorie elasticity as
\(\varepsilon\) or as \(\sigma\).

\section{How we identify substitution without fitting 2008}
\label{sec:ident}

For each \(\sigma\) in the pre-declared band we run the same three
Agrimate-style legs Gate~0 used, now on the coupled spine: harvest-only,
AMIS-only, and official full (harvest plus AMIS, mean flex, maize ethanol
on). Price metrics are the same: monthly correlation with Pink Sheet, and
a hike ratio (3-month mean around the crisis peak divided by 3-month mean
around a pre-crisis base). For 2007/08 that is June 2006 versus March
2008; for 2010/11, June 2009 versus February 2011; for 2021/22, June 2021
versus May 2022.

\textbf{Preferred claim:} report all three \(\sigma\). No point estimate.

\textbf{If a preferred \(\sigma\) were required:} select only on the
2007/08 \textbf{wheat\(\to\)maize} margin. Hold out rice 2008, plus
2010/11, plus 2022. Training on rice 2008 is withdrawn: that episode is an
own-ban, not a clean substitution fingerprint (\S\ref{sec:data}). This
paper does not exercise that option. The band is the result.

Fitting 2008 then claiming validation includes: maximizing 2007/08
rice/maize fit then reporting that window as the result; joint \(\sigma\)
on 2008+2010+2022; retuning \texttt{CropParams} / \(\rho\) / Gate~0
leftovers to help spillovers; peeking at coupled scores then declaring the
grid. None of that is done here.

\subsection{Hard bars (every reported \(\sigma>0\))}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Identity.} Coupled \(\sigma=0\), official split: monthly
      world price of each grain matches the corresponding
      \texttt{run\_crop\_dynamics} to relative error \(\lesssim 0.5\%\).
\item \textbf{Spillover sign.} In 2007/08, \(\sigma>0\) must not
      \emph{lower} rice and maize hikes relative to \(\sigma=0\) while
      wheat is up. A fall is a bug or a wrong \(\rho\) sign.
\item \textbf{Wheat Gate~0 not wrecked.} 2007/08 stays restriction-led and
      2010/11 stays production-led (isolated-leg signs). Amplitude may
      move; a sign flip is a fail.
\item \textbf{Rice still has its own ban.} Isolated rice AMIS must still
      carry most of the 2008 rice hike. Substitution is a spillover, not a
      replacement for India/Vietnam \(\tau\).
\end{enumerate}

Soft \(\Delta\)corr / \(\Delta\)hike on hold-outs are reported, not used
to pick \(\sigma^\star\). A pass must not invent a rice hike in 2010/11
(observed ratio \(<1\)). World consumption versus PSD is a diagnostic, not
a selection target (\S\ref{sec:cons}).

co-movement as the least-confounded spillover; rice 2008 = own-policy
(hard bar 4); rice 2010 = no fake co-spike. Do not require all three
series to look equally pretty.

\section{Diagnostic: world consumption versus PSD}
\label{sec:cons}

Gate~0 already failed to track USDA consumption \emph{trends}: official
split is mean flex plus \(\varepsilon<0\), so when price spikes, use
falls, and there is no year-by-year food/feed forcing in the headline.
World mean ratios were \(\times 0.86\)--\(0.92\) with negative
correlations. That leftover is not a retune target, and substitution is
not a license to hide it.

Table~\ref{tab:cons} scores the same object on the frozen band. At

wrong. Do not pick \(\sigma\) from these ratios. Do not reopen the
warehouse.

\section{What this paper can say}
\label{sec:claim}

This is what substitution on the locked Gate~0 spine can say. It is not a
fitted \(\sigma^\star\). Agrimate cannot do this layer~\citep{kuhla2025}.

\paragraph{Can claim.}
Coupled isoelastic food/feed demand with \(\sigma=0\) recovers the
single-crop Gate~0 prices (2006--11 and 2021--23) to machine precision.
In 2007/08, turning \(\sigma\) up raises rice and maize hikes relative to
\(\sigma=0\) (the substitution sign). Wheat stays restriction-led in 2008
and production-led in 2010 at every reported \(\sigma>0\). Isolated rice
AMIS still carries essentially all of the 2008 rice hike; substitution is a
spillover, not a replacement for India/Vietnam bans. In 2021--23 wheat
stays AMIS-led and maize stays flat.

\paragraph{Must disclose.}
\(\sigma\in\{0,0.3,0.6\}\) was declared before looking at coupled scores.
Rice 2008 in Pink Sheet is primarily an own-ban episode, not a clean
wheat\(\to\)rice test. The 2010 maize hike-\emph{ratio} drop at
\(\sigma=0.6\) is a higher June 2009 base after the 2008 wheat--maize
spillover, not a lower 2011 peak. In 2022 rice drifts up
(\(\times 1.18\)) against a falling Pink Sheet (\(\times 0.91\)). Dual
host: this layer is not annual Slutsky \(M_i\). Wheat 2008 overshoot gets
a bit worse as \(\sigma\) rises. Gate~0 consumption leftovers are
inherited: mean ratios tick up a little, maize use-correlation gets worse,
and \(\Delta\)cons signs do not move. Shipment leftovers are untouched.

\paragraph{Must not claim.}
That \(\sigma=0.6\) (or \(0.3\)) is estimated. That matching the 2008 rice
spike validates substitution. That 2022 wheat corr (\(0.69\to 0.75\))
selects \(\sigma\). That Gate~0 consumption/shipment leftovers are fixed.
That governments choose bans (that is later work on this spine, not this note).

\section{What still misses, on purpose}
\label{sec:leftovers}

These are not a to-do list for the next parameter tweak.

\begin{itemize}
\item Wheat 2007/08 still high, and substitution pushes it higher
      (\(\times 2.27\to\times 2.45\) versus observed \(\times 1.82\)).
\item Maize 2010 hike \emph{ratio} at \(\sigma=0.6\) looks like a collapse
      because 2009 is already expensive (\S\ref{sec:e5}).
\item 2022 rice drifts the wrong way (\(\times 0.99\to\times 1.18\) versus
      \(\times 0.91\)).
\item Gate~0 consumption path versus PSD trend. Substitution ticks mean
      ratios up a little and makes maize's correlation worse
      (\(-0.64\to -0.84\)); \(\Delta\)cons signs do not move
      (\S\ref{sec:cons}).
\item Gate~0 AMIS shipment \emph{levels} versus PSD; prescribed FAOSTAT E0.
\item Dual host: isoelastic \(\eta\) here, linear \(M_i\) in the annual
      SPE. Not equated.
\item No convenience yield / futures smoothing.
\item Ukraine 2022: AMIS classes only; wartime logistics outside AMIS are
      not in \(\tau\).
\item Governments do not choose bans.
\end{itemize}

None of these is a license to fit 2008. Densifying \(\{0,0.3,0.6\}\),
picking \(\sigma^\star\) because wheat 2022 corr rose, retuning
\texttt{CropParams} or \(\rho\), unpausing \texttt{dynamic\_grains.py}, or
starting the export game would be a different paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

Gate~1 is complete enough to write down. On the locked Gate~0 spine, with
governments still not choosing bans, isoelastic food/feed substitution at
a pre-declared \(\sigma\) band recovers single-crop prices when switching
is off, spills in the substitute direction in 2007/08, leaves wheat's
2008/2010 attribution signs and rice's own-ban dominance intact, and does
not invent a 2022 maize spike. It does not estimate \(\sigma\), does not
turn 2008 rice into a substitution test, does not fix Gate~0 consumption
or shipment leftovers, and does not hide a 2010 maize hike-ratio leftover
that is really a 2009 level. Those sentences are the result. The next
paper is the export game, on this spine, not instead of it --- and not
until someone asks for it.
