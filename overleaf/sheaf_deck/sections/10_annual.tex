\begin{frame}{Linear demand and the benefit potential}
\topic{Annual leftover demand: linear in prices, with a consumer-surplus potential.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S1}{core.py:79--87}

\[
D_i = a_i - M_i p_i,
\qquad
p_i = M_i^{-1}(a_i - D_i),
\]
\[
W_i(D_i) = (M_i^{-1}a_i)^\top D_i - \tfrac12 D_i^\top M_i^{-1} D_i,
\qquad
\nabla_{D_i} W_i = p_i,
\]
\[
CS_i = W_i(D_i) - p_i^\top D_i.
\]

$M_i$ symmetric positive-definite $\Rightarrow$ $W_i$ strictly concave
$\Rightarrow$ the QP is well-posed. Code: \texttt{DemandSystem.surplus}.
\syms{$D$ consumption (MMT/year) \symsep $p$ node price (\$/t) \symsep $a$ choke demand (MMT) \symsep $M$ slope (MMT/(\$/t)) \symsep $W,CS$ surplus ((\$/t)$\times$MMT).}
\bubbles{$M_i$ constructed next slide. \texttt{subst\_scale} default $0.6$ on this host.}
{$D$: MMT/year. $p$: \$/t. $W$: (\$/t)$\times$MMT.}
{\texttt{core.build\_demand\_system}; README \S1.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Construction of $M_i$}
\topic{How the leftover demand-slope matrix is built from elasticities.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S1}{core.py:111--130}

{\small
\[
b_g = -\varepsilon_g \frac{D_{0,g}}{p_{0,g}}>0,
\qquad
S_{gh}=\sigma\rho_{gh}\sqrt{b_g b_h}\ (g\neq h),\quad S_{gg}=0,
\]
\[
\text{row-cap row }g\text{ if }\textstyle\sum_h S_{gh}\ge 0.95 b_g;
\quad
S\leftarrow\sqrt{S\circ S^\top};
\quad
M=\mathrm{diag}(b)-S;
\quad
a=D_0+M p_0.
\]}

The audit-prep note asked whether the arithmetic symmetrisation
$S\leftarrow 0.5(S+S^\top)$ preserves the construction; the code in fact
uses the \textbf{geometric} mean, which is what keeps the $0.95$ row bound.
\syms{$g,h$ grains \symsep $b_g$ own demand slope (MMT per \$/t, $>0$)
\symsep $S_{gh}$ off-diagonal substitution block and $M$ the demand-slope
matrix (both MMT per \$/t) \symsep $a$ choke demand and $D_{0}$ baseline
consumption (MMT/year) \symsep $p_0$ reference price (\$/t)
\symsep $\varepsilon_g$ own elasticity, $\sigma$ substitution scale,
$\rho_{gh}$ pairwise substitutability (all dimensionless)
\symsep $\circ$ elementwise product, $\sqrt{\cdot}$ elementwise root.
Here $\varepsilon$ is \texttt{calibration.OWN\_ELAST}, \emph{not}
\texttt{CropParams.elast}.}
\bubbles{$\sigma=\mathtt{subst\_scale}$. $\varepsilon$ here is \texttt{OWN\_ELAST}=$(-0.25,-0.20,-0.30)$, \emph{not} CropParams.}
{$b$: MMT / (\$/t). $M$: same. $a$: MMT.}
{\texttt{calibration.OWN\_ELAST}, \texttt{P0}, \texttt{RHO}; \texttt{core.py:126}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{$M\succ 0$ on the calibration}
\topic{The leftover demand matrix is positive definite on the calibration. Diagonal dominance is only a construction step.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S1: dominance is a construction device}{core.py:121--128}

Rows of $S$ are capped for dominance \emph{before} the geometric-mean
symmetrisation. After that step some nodes do lose strict diagonal
dominance: at $\sigma=0.6$, 11 of 18 nodes hit the $0.95$ row cap and the
worst dominance margin is $-1.07\times 10^{-3}$ --- yet the smallest
eigenvalue is still $+1.44\times 10^{-4}$.

So the PD claim \textbf{holds} on all 18 calibration nodes at
$\sigma\in\{0,0.3,0.6,0.9\}$, and 20{,}000 randomised draws
($G=2..5$, own slopes over six orders of magnitude, $\sigma\in[0.1,10]$)
produced no counterexample. README \S1 states this: dominance is a
construction device, not a surviving certificate. Leftover host --- not
the crisis spine.
\syms{$M$ leftover demand-slope matrix (MMT per \$/t) \symsep $S$ its
off-diagonal substitution block (MMT per \$/t) \symsep $\sigma$ leftover
substitution scale (dimensionless) \symsep $G$ number of grains \symsep
PD = positive definite (all eigenvalues $>0$) \symsep dominance margin
$=|M_{gg}|-\sum_{h\neq g}|M_{gh}|$, in MMT per \$/t.}
\bubbles{No extra cap after symmetrisation.}
{Eigenvalues of $M$: MMT/(\$/t).}
{\texttt{verify\_claims.py} section 8; README \S1.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Market QP}
\topic{The leftover market: a yearly spatial QP. An export tax here is dollars per tonne.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S2}{core.SpatialEquilibrium.solve}

\[
\max_{D\ge 0,\, f\ge 0}
\sum_i W_i(D_i) - \sum_{g,i,j} K^g_{ij} f^g_{ij}
\]
\[
A^g_i + \sum_k f^g_{ki} - \sum_k f^g_{ik} - D^g_i = 0,
\quad
f^g_{ii}=0,
\]
\[
K^g_{ij} = c_{ij}\phi_g\psi_{ij} + \tau^g_i + m^g_j.
\]

$c_{ij}$ is haversine $\times 0.0025 + 8$ (\$/t). $\phi$ is
\texttt{FREIGHT\_MULT}. Solver fallback CLARABEL $\to$ SCS $\to$ OSQP;
status must be \texttt{optimal} (not merely $D.\mathrm{value}$ not None).
\syms{$D$ consumption (MMT/year) \symsep $f$ bilateral flow (MMT/year) \symsep $A$ availability (MMT) \symsep $K,c,\tau,m$ \$/t \symsep $W$ surplus.}
\bubbles{$\tau$ here is \$/t, not an offer fraction.}
{$f$: MMT/year. $K$: \$/t.}
{\texttt{core.py:292--351}; \texttt{calibration.\_haversine}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{KKT / spatial arbitrage}
\topic{Grain moves on a leftover route only if the price gap covers shipping plus the tax.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S2 Enke--Samuelson}{QP KKT, not separately coded}

At an optimum the multiplier on the balance is the recovered inverse-demand
price, and complementary slackness is

\[
p^g_j - p^g_i \le K^g_{ij},
\qquad
f^g_{ij}\bigl(K^g_{ij}-(p^g_j-p^g_i)\bigr)=0.
\]

Grain moves only when the gap covers delivered cost. An export tax raises
every route out of $i$. This is the insulation mechanism on the leftover
host. The crisis host insulates by cutting \textbf{offers}, not by a $K$ wedge.
\syms{$p$ node price (\$/t) \symsep $K$ delivered cost plus tax (\$/t) \symsep $f$ flow (MMT/year).}
\bubbles{No extra KKT parameter.}
{\$ / t on the gap.}
{README \S2; cvxpy KKT of the QP in \texttt{SpatialEquilibrium.solve}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Competitive storage --- lagged price, disclosed}
\topic{Leftover commercial storage uses last year's price, not a rational-expectations storage equilibrium.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S3}{core.market\_responsive\_storage}

\[
p^e = p^{\mathrm{prev}} + \kappa\bigl(p^{\mathrm{target}}-p^{\mathrm{prev}}\bigr),
\quad
s = \frac{p^e}{1+r}-p^{\mathrm{prev}},
\]
\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{mkt}}
=
\begin{cases}
\gamma(s-\theta\,\mathrm{sgn}\,s) & |s|>\theta,\\
0 & |s|\le\theta,
\end{cases}
\quad\text{clipped to }[-R,\,\bar R-R].
\]

$p^{\mathrm{ref}}$ is last year's realised price --- a one-period lag,
disclosed, not simultaneous TWIST/Agrimate storage. Not Deaton--Laroque
RE. Category \textbf{E}.
\syms{$p^e,p^{\mathrm{prev}}$ \$/t \symsep $\Delta^{\mathrm{mkt}}$ private stock change (MMT/year) \symsep $R$ stock (MMT) \symsep $r,\kappa$ dimensionless \symsep $\theta$ \$/t.}
\bubbles{$r=0.05$, $\kappa=0.5$, $\theta=\mathtt{mkt\_cost}=8$ \$/t default.}

{\texttt{core.py:195--206, 474--478}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Government buffer on the leftover host}
\topic{Leftover government buffer: release in a crisis, rebuild in calm. Not the Gate~2 rule.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S3}{core.strategic\_storage}

\[
\Sigma = D_0 - A^{\mathrm{pre-gov}} - \max(D_0-Q_0,0).

\begin{frame}{Annual export-restriction game}
\topic{The leftover export game: an annual tax in dollars per tonne, not the fortnight cut.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S4}{core.ExportRestrictionGame}

\[
\mathcal{W}_i
= CS_i + \underbrace{p_i\cdot Q_i}_{\Pi_i\text{ uses baseline }Q}
- \sum_g w_{i,g}(p_{i,g}-\bar p_{i,g})_+^2
+ \zeta \tau_i\cdot X_i.
\]

Nash by iterated best response on a grid $\{0,\ldots,\bar\tau\}$
(default $\bar\tau=120$ \$/t, \texttt{grid=13}, \texttt{max\_iters=3},
\texttt{tol=3} \$/t). Non-concave $\mathcal{W}$ $\Rightarrow$ approximate /
discretised equilibrium, as README says.

Producer income uses unshocked $Q$, not realised sales --- intentional
prototype weight. Stress gate: game plays only if
$\max p > \mu p^{\mathrm{norm}}$ ($\mu=1.12$).
\syms{$\mathcal{W}$ welfare \symsep $CS$ consumer surplus \symsep $p$ \$/t \symsep $Q,X$ MMT/year \symsep $\tau$ \$/t tax (not an offer fraction) \symsep $w$ \$ weights.}
\bubbles{$w_{i,g}$, $\bar p_{i,g}$ from illustrative \texttt{DATA}. $\zeta=0$ default.}
{$\tau$: \$/t. $\mathcal{W}$: (\$/t)$\times$MMT.}
{\texttt{core.py:357--421}; stress gate \texttt{core.py:506--512}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{README \S6 proposition --- holds on this host}
\topic{On the leftover host, turning substitution off splits the yearly problem into three separate markets.}
\leftoverbanner{README \S6}{core.build\_demand\_system at $\sigma=0$}

If $\sigma=0$ (equivalently $\rho\equiv 0$), $S=0$ and $M_i=\mathrm{diag}(b)$
exactly (verified $\max|M_{\mathrm{off}}|=0$). $W_i$ separates, the QP
splits into $G$ independent SPEs, and $\mathcal{W}_i$ decouples. That is
the TWIST/Agrimate-style limit of the \textbf{annual} host.

The live-host analogue is Gate~1's $\sigma=0$ identity, already shown.
\texttt{demo.py} measures substitution spillover on this leftover host
(Black Sea wheat shock). It is not a 2007/08 hindcast.
\syms{$\sigma$ leftover substitution scale. $M=\mathrm{diag}(b)$ at $\sigma=0$ (MMT/(\$/t)). Live-host analogue: Gate~1 $\sigma=0$.}
\bubbles{\texttt{build\_countries(substitution=False)} sets $\sigma=0$.}
{Same units as \S1.}
{\texttt{verify\_claims.py} PD section; \texttt{demo.py}.}
\end{frame}
