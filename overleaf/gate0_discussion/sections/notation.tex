\section{Symbols, units, and parameter classes}
\label{sec:notation}

Countries are indexed by \(i\) (importer \(j\) where a bilateral pair is
needed) and two-week periods by \(t\), with \(\Delta t\approx 15.2\) days
and exactly two periods per calendar month. Quantities are in million
tonnes (MMT); flows are MMT per period. Prices are in constant 2010 US
dollars per tonne. Shares are dimensionless and sum to one over the
relevant index.

One notational collision is worth flagging: \(S_{i,t}\) denotes a
\emph{stock} in MMT, while \(S_{ij}\) denotes the FAOSTAT \emph{source
share} of importer \(j\) from exporter \(i\), a dimensionless fraction.
The trade matrices supply bilateral structure only; all traded volumes are
determined by the offer and import-demand equations.

Every symbol below is also defined in place at the equation where it first
appears, so \S\ref{sec:dynamics} can be read without returning here.

\subsection{Parameters and current values}

Parameters are classified as \textbf{L} (literature-sourced: elasticities,
stock-to-use norms), \textbf{S} (definitional --- part of the model's
specification, such as the clock, the restriction mapping, and the
calendars), or \textbf{R} (reduced-form: the sign is economically
motivated, the magnitude is not a structural elasticity, and the value is
not retuned crisis by crisis).

Parameters carrying the unit ``per step'' are gains in the discrete
two-week update. They are not continuous-time rates and should not be
divided by \(\Delta t\) to obtain a physical frequency.
