\begin{abstract}
SHEAF (Substitution, Heterogeneous agents, Equilibrium, And Fragility) is
meant, eventually, as a multi-crop model in which governments choose export
bans. Those extras are switched off here. This note is Gate~0: one grain at
a time, a step every two weeks, historical export bans taken from a policy
diary, no switching from wheat to rice when wheat is expensive, no government
choosing a ban. We write the mathematics in full, say where every number
came from, and map equations to code. Scoring follows Agrimate's idea of
three runs---a normal harvest, a harvest with shocks, and shocks plus the
real bans---with average food-and-feed demand and, for maize, a US ethanol
mandate leftover. World prices are compared to World Bank Pink Sheet series
in constant 2010 dollars, not Agrimate's CPI-deflated levels. On 2006--11
the matched run correlates $+0.72$ (wheat), $+0.71$ (maize), $+0.68$ (rice)
with monthly Pink Sheet; 2007/08 wheat and rice spikes are driven by export
bans, maize 2007/08 by US ethanol demand, and 2010/11 wheat by the harvest
after we remove the slow yield trend. A 2021--23 Ukraine-war run with the
same parameters reproduces the wheat spike ($\times 1.61$ versus observed
$\times 1.57$), almost entirely from recorded bans (India's May 2022 wheat
ban and Russia's March 2022 measures), while maize and rice barely moved in
the data and the model does not invent a spike. World stocks at the end of
each marketing year match USDA to $\times 1.02$--$1.08$; the path of
consumption and some export \emph{volumes} do not. Those leftovers are left
as leftovers. Turning on substitution is the next paper, not a way to hide
them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
\label{sec:scope}

SHEAF's destination is a country-level model of wheat, rice, and maize in
which governments choose export restrictions and consumers can switch among
grains when one of them spikes~\citep{kuhla2025,schewe2017}. That is not this
paper. Before those extras can be tested, each crop's market has to work on
its own: prices should move in the right months, stocks should be compared
at the month USDA actually reports, and historical bans should cut export
offers in the countries that actually banned.

This note is that prior claim (Gate~0 / paper P1). It does four things.

\begin{enumerate}
\item State the Gate~0 mathematics without remainder
      (\S\ref{sec:math}), including every parameterization choice and
      what was tried and rejected (\S\ref{sec:params}).
\item Say, in one place, what is copied from Agrimate and what is not
      (\S\ref{sec:vs-agrimate}).
\item Score locked parameters on 2007/08, 2010/11, and the 2022 Ukraine-war
      window (\S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:ukraine}), substitution off
      and strategy off.
\item Place those scores next to TWIST and Agrimate on objects they
      actually published (\S\ref{sec:compare}).
\end{enumerate}

A supplement maps every equation to functions in
\texttt{sheaf/dynamic\_crop.py} (\S\ref{sec:code}). An older annual
market-clearing program in \texttt{sheaf/core.py} (spatial price equilibrium
plus a once-per-year export game) is a leftover prototype from when the
market itself was annual. It is not the crisis game and it is not run
here~\citep{takayama1971,samuelson1952,headey2011}. The crisis game, when
it is switched on, lives on this 24-step spine: types are slow, actions
$\tau_t$ respond to conditions.

\textbf{What is switched off.} Consumers cannot substitute wheat for rice
or maize. Export cuts are a historical diary, not a choice governments
make inside the model. Who trades with whom is taken from FAOSTAT, not
grown from shipping costs. We do not add special knobs for 2008 or 2010.

\textbf{What is claimed.} With those restrictions, the spine is a
stock--trade--selling-price system whose pieces can be explained, whose
tightness weights keep the same sign every year, and whose official
hindcast is identified the way Agrimate identifies it, plus a documented
US maize ethanol leftover. We do not claim a textbook competitive-storage
equilibrium, a gallon-by-gallon ethanol series, or Agrimate's private CPI
price path.

\subsection{A short glossary}

\begin{description}
\item[Ask] The selling price an exporter posts this fortnight. Raise it
      when the shipment sold out; cut it when grain sat unsold.
\item[Offer] Tonnes the exporter is willing to put on the water after
      feeding people and keeping a hungry-season buffer, then after any
      ban or tax.
\item[AMIS] The G20/OECD diary of real export policies (bans, quotas,
      taxes). We map labels to a fraction of the offer, not to observed
      tonnes.
\item[Armington] Importers prefer their usual suppliers rather than
      treating all origins as one pile of identical grain.
\item[Twin] A calm-weather copy of the same model (normal harvest, no
      bans, no ethanol) used as a baseline. If the crisis run matches it,
      the world price target is the reference price.
\item[Pink Sheet] World Bank monthly commodity prices. Ours are in
      constant 2010 dollars (MUV deflator).
\item[PSD] USDA Production, Supply and Distribution balances
      (harvests, use, ending stocks).
\item[Matched / official split] The headline run: harvest shocks plus
      AMIS bans, average food-and-feed demand, US maize ethanol on.
\end{description}

\section{Lineage: TWIST $\to$ Agrimate $\to$ SHEAF}
\label{sec:lineage}

TWIST~\citep{schewe2017} reproduces annual world wheat prices from a
stylised price--supply curve and storage dynamics. It does not resolve the
bilateral trade network or export restrictions. Falkendal et al.\
\citep{falkendal2021} use that lineage for COVID-era restriction scenarios.
Agrimate~\citep{kuhla2025} adds a 24-step-per-year agent-based network with
commercial and strategic stockholding, adaptive exporter offers, and
historical AMIS restrictions taken as given. Wheat is the main-text
hindcast (2006--11); rice, maize, and soybean appear as separate
single-commodity calibrations in Supplement~G. Agrimate's published checks
are world prices versus a CPI-deflated Pink Sheet, global supply and stock
\emph{changes}, a 2007 regional wheat map (28 regions), a Russia--Egypt
mechanism case, and a 2022 wheat exercise (Suppl.\ Fig.~A.5). Agrimate is
explicitly single-commodity.

SHEAF exists to put two things on that network that Agrimate does not:
consumers switching among grains, and governments choosing restrictions
rather than following a diary. Gate~0 asks whether the network-plus-storage
spine, \emph{without} those two things, already hindcasts prices, stocks,
and restriction signs. If it does not, turning substitution on cannot be
read as a test of substitution.

Food-trade fragility work~\citep{puma2015} motivates looking at who buys
from whom. Export restrictions as a way to keep domestic prices down are
why the AMIS diary exists~\citep{headey2011,martin2012,wright2011}.
Headey (2011) is also why those restrictions, import surges, and stock
\emph{announcements} have dates rather than marketing years: India and
Vietnam in October 2007, Thailand discussing a ban in March 2008, Japan
allowed to re-export rice stocks in May. Characteristic government types
can be sticky; the actions are not. The annual spatial price
equilibrium~\citep{enke1951,samuelson1952,takayama1971} remains a
prototype on \texttt{sheaf/core.py}. Gate~0 matches offers to usual
suppliers every fortnight, because that is the clock Agrimate uses and
the clock Headey's events occupy. Later papers that let governments
\emph{choose} $\tau$ do it on this spine, not by returning to an annual
Nash. They do not require Gate~0 to be re-run.

\section{How Gate~0 differs from Agrimate}
\label{sec:vs-agrimate}

Gate~0 is deliberately Agrimate-like: one crop, two-week steps, historical
bans taken as given. It is not a reimplementation of Agrimate, and it
should not be read as ``we recoded their agents.'' This section lists the
design differences. Score differences (who carried 2008 maize, how fat
stocks are) are in \S\ref{sec:compare}.

\subsection{What we copy}

The clock (24 steps per year). Harvest shocks as deviations from a slow
local trend, not from a raw six-year mean. Export policy as an AMIS diary
with bans taking most of the offer and taxes taking about half. The three
runs used to tell harvest stories from ban stories: a normal harvest, a
shocked harvest, and a shocked harvest plus the real bans. A bilateral
trade network rather than one world pool. Exporters who post selling
prices and adjust them. Storage as an operational buffer, not a
rational-expectations commodity-storage equilibrium. Wheat as the
main-text bar; rice and maize scored the same way, as Agrimate's
Supplement~G does. The 2022 wheat episode as a hold-out of sorts
(their Suppl.\ Fig.~A.5; our \S\ref{sec:ukraine}).

\subsection{Where the models are not the same}

\paragraph{The quiet baseline.}
Agrimate's unperturbed run still moves with the harvest calendar and
storage cost. Ours is an identity: if nothing is shocked and nobody
restricts, the world price target is the reference price, so the grey
line in our figures is flat. Comparing grey baselines visually is
therefore unfair. Compare how the crisis run peels away from its own
baseline.

\paragraph{What ``world price'' is.}
Agrimate's published series is an export-weighted offer price from an
agent-based commercial layer (and they deflate Pink Sheet with US CPI).
Ours is mostly the tonnage-weighted average of posted asks, plus a
smaller tightness residual against the twin, in MUV 2010 dollars. A
SHEAF peak near \$400/t and an Agrimate peak near \$200/t are not a
forecast error; they are different deflators. We compare hike
\emph{ratios} and monthly correlation, not dollar levels.

\paragraph{Demand.}
Agrimate lets consumption emerge from agents. Official Gate~0 holds
food-and-feed use at its 2006--11 average (so a price spike can still
cut use through the elasticity, but the baseline diet does not trend)
and, for maize only, adds US ethanol as a mandate leftover. That ethanol
block is why our 2008 maize story is ``industrial demand'' and theirs is
``restrictions on a stable harvest.'' Both can be true of their models;
they are not the same identification. Feeding year-by-year PSD diets is
a sensitivity we report, not their Fig.~4.

\paragraph{Who is on the map.}
Agrimate publishes 28-region wheat maps for 2007. We have 17 named
countries plus Rest-of-World. We cannot redraw their map. Node-level
bars versus USDA are the substitute.

\paragraph{Silos.}
Agrimate distinguishes commercial and strategic stockholding with more
agent detail. We have one stock per country, a hungry-season target, a
carry ceiling, and a wheat/maize pipeline pad. Rice gets no extra
pipeline pad, because year-round harvest was being stored as if it were
silos. One selling price per exporter: we do not split a cheap domestic
offer from a dear export offer.

\paragraph{Trade stickiness.}
Both models use a network. Ours takes FAOSTAT shares from a declared
window and keeps them. Agrimate's agents are richer. Neither Gate~0 nor
Agrimate grows a new network from freight costs (that is SHEAF paper P2).
When a ban hits, our importers only partly reroute through a 15\% leftover
pool; leftover shipment \emph{volumes} versus USDA are often too high,
which we list rather than patch.

\paragraph{What we score that they did not.}
World consumption \emph{levels} versus USDA (their Fig.~4 is harvest and
stock \emph{changes}). Export-offer and shipment signs with bans on versus
off. World stocks with China taken out, because FAO/AMIS tightness talk
is about grain that can actually reach the market. Those extras make
Gate~0 look worse than a price-only score; that is intended.

\paragraph{What they score that we do not.}
Their Supplement~F parameter sensitivity. Soybean. A seasonal unperturbed
price. CPI-deflated HRW levels. We do not claim those.

\paragraph{What neither of us does in this comparison.}
Cross-grain substitution (Agrimate is single-crop; Gate~0 switches it
off). Governments choosing bans (both take AMIS as given). That common
limit is the point of Gate~0: isolate the market spine before SHEAF's
own extras.

\section{Where the numbers come from}
\label{sec:params}

Table~\ref{tab:params} is the default parameter set. Every number is one of
three kinds. \textbf{Structural} means a data definition or a lineage
choice (the two-week clock, the AMIS diary map, US maize ethanol as a
mandate leftover). Changing it is a redesign, not a fit. \textbf{Literature}
means a published elasticity or a typical calm-year stock-to-use, not
something we backed out of 2008 prices. \textbf{Reduced-form} means a
weight that links a measured tightness (how empty the world's free grain
is, whether usual suppliers are blocked) to price. Those weights keep a
required sign --- tighter markets raise prices --- and they are the same
in 2008 and 2010. They are not structural first-order conditions.

\paragraph{Structural (not free).}
The two-week clock, harvest calendars, the slow-trend harvest smoother, the
AMIS diary map, who trades with whom (including the Vietnam rice repair),
US maize ethanol as a leftover above 2000--04, rice without extra pipeline
silos, and the rule that a quiet twin sits at the reference price. Changing
them is a redesign, not a calibration step.

\paragraph{Literature.}
How much food-and-feed use shrinks when price rises sits in the USDA ERS
range for staples~\citep{muhammad2011}: wheat the least responsive of the
three, maize more so through feed. Those elasticities are not estimated on
2008 prices. Target and maximum stocks as a share of annual use are the
order of USDA world ratios in calm years ($\sim$0.15--0.28), not fitted
peaks.
Roberts and Schlenker~\citep{roberts2013} identify calorie supply/demand
and the ethanol mandate at a different aggregation; we use their
\emph{qualitative} implication (US maize industrial demand is a mandate,
not an isoelastic consumer) via the FSI residual~\eqref{eq:industrial},
not their calorie elasticity as $\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{Reduced-form, sign-constrained.}
$\omega,\eta,\kappa_u,\kappa_b,\lambda,\rho$ and the ask gains link
measured tightness (free-stock ratio, unmet anomaly, preferred-source
blockage, fill rates) to price. Signs are required by the economics
(tighter $\Rightarrow$ higher $p$; sold-out $\Rightarrow$ higher ask;
surplus $\Rightarrow$ lower $p$). Magnitudes are shared across years.
\texttt{ask\_rival}$=0.80$ was set by the isolated-maize-$\tau$ non-cut
assert, not by 2008 peaks. We do not pretend these are structural FOCs.

\paragraph{Tried and rejected (same spine, not hidden knobs).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{In-sample mean harvest levels} treated 2008--11 trend growth
      as a 2006 shortfall. Replaced by climatology $\times$ LOWESS
      (\S\ref{sec:harvest}).
\item \textbf{Country-specific price-responsive commercial hold} fattened
      China maize $\sim\times 3$ while helping USA wheat. Reverted to one
      world STU floor. Exporter nodes (USA/Canada/Australia wheat) still
      sit near that floor ($\sim\times 0.2$ versus PSD).
\item \textbf{Hard warehouse dump} incinerated harvest-shoulder grain.
      Replaced by soft clip at $\lambda$.
\item \textbf{Rice $H_t$ padding $W$} stored the monsoon as silos; world
      December STU was $\times 1.63$. \texttt{pipeline=0} and scoring at
      August MY-end brought world stocks to $\times 1.05$.
\item \textbf{Crisis dummies} (boost $\kappa_b$ only in 2010, mute 2008
      wheat, etc.) are forbidden. Leftovers in \S\ref{sec:leftovers}
      stay leftovers.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Numeraire.}
2006--11 uses $p_0=$ mean 2006 real Pink Sheet so the twin sits on the
pre-crisis year. 2021--23 uses 2021 mean so ask clips
$[0.45p_0,2.8p_0]$ are local to that window. Reduced-form gains are
ratio-valued and are not retuned. Pink Sheet in SHEAF is MUV 2010
dollars~\citep{worldbank_pink}. Agrimate Fig.~4a uses US-CPI deflated HRW
(their peak near \$200/t versus SHEAF's real peak near \$400/t). Dollar
\emph{levels} are not a target; hike ratios and monthly correlation are.

\section{How we tell harvest stories from ban stories}
\label{sec:ident}

For each crop we run four variants, then compare them. This is Agrimate's
idea, plus an extra sensitivity.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{baseline} --- a normal seasonal harvest, no bans, no ethanol.
      The twin. Price should sit at the reference $p_0$.
\item \textbf{shocks} --- real harvest highs and lows, no bans; maize
      ethanol on. If this run spikes, the harvest did it.
\item \textbf{tau} --- a normal harvest, but the real AMIS bans on, ethanol
      off. If this run spikes, the bans did it.
\item \textbf{full} --- harvest shocks plus bans, average food-and-feed,
      maize ethanol on. This is the official headline.
\end{enumerate}

Feeding each year's actual PSD food-and-feed (\texttt{use\_demand=True}) is
reported as a sensitivity. Agrimate's published full run does not force
that series; their consumption comes from agents.

\textbf{Price.} We report monthly correlation with Pink Sheet, and a hike
ratio: the 3-month mean around the crisis peak divided by the 3-month mean
around a pre-crisis base. For 2007/08 that is June 2006 versus March 2008;
for 2010/11, June 2009 versus February 2011. ``Restriction-led'' means the
bans-only run moved more than the harvest-only run. It is not a Shapley
decomposition.

\textbf{Stocks.} USDA ending stocks are a marketing-year number, not a
calendar December snapshot. We compare world stocks in the month that
matches that crop's USDA year (wheat May, maize August, rice
\textbf{August}). Each country is then compared at its own USDA month.
``World excluding China'' is the same month with China's pile removed,
because a lot of China grain never reaches the traded market. China stays
in the model as a named country.

\textbf{Consumption.} Calendar-year use versus the sum of our countries'
USDA use --- not the world PSD file, which omits the EU. Agrimate Fig.~4
is harvest and stock \emph{changes}, not consumption levels. This bar is
extra, and it is not green.

\textbf{Trade.} The fair check is: with bans on, did the restricting
country offer (and, where we can, ship) \emph{less} than in the harvest-only
run? Agrimate's Fig.~3 is grain the model says was withheld, not FAOSTAT
crisis trade. Comparing annual model exports to USDA tonnes is a level
check we also print; several of those levels fail. For maize, turning
bans on by themselves must not \emph{cut} the 14-month mean world price
(one May 2007 fortnight may dip; the mean must not).

\textbf{Asserts} (all PASS on the locked parameters): the quiet twin stays
flat; AMIS raises wheat and rice prices in the main ban window; AMIS cuts
the designated exporter's offers (wheat: also shipments); a normal-harvest
year has no fake spring price spike.

\paragraph{Wheat.}
2007/08 is restriction-led ($\tau\times 1.70$ versus harvest $\times 1.20$).
The full hike $\times 2.27$ overshoots observed $\times 1.82$; we do not
damp it with a 2008 dummy. 2010/11 is production-led after detrend
(harvest $\times 1.85$). The June~2009--February~2011 ratio also lifts 2009
via lingering AMIS, so restriction looks weaker on the ratio than in
levels. Agrimate's narrative treats 2007 as more production-led than we
do; we do not relabel 2008 to match them. Monthly correlation $+0.72$ is
the highest of the three crops.

\paragraph{Maize.}
2007/08 full $\times 1.97$ versus observed $\times 1.84$. Isolated harvest
and isolated AMIS are both $\sim\times 1.1$; year-by-year demand is
$\times 1.46$. The official full's extra lift is the USA industrial
block interacting with harvest and AMIS. That is a different story from
Agrimate Suppl.~G.2 (restriction-led 2008 on a stable harvest). Both can
be true of their respective models; they are not the same identification.
Isolated $\tau\times 1.10$ does not cut the 14-month mean --- the maize
assert that forbids reversing the AMIS channel. 2010/11 full $\times 1.70$
versus $\times 1.44$ is high; harvest-only is $\times 1.01$.

\paragraph{Rice.}
2008 $\times 1.72$ versus $\times 1.84$, $\tau\times 1.75$, harvest

($\times 0.82$ versus $\times 0.79$). Forcing year-by-year demand
\emph{worsens} 2010 (sensitivity $\times 1.60$). Headline rice therefore
keeps mean flex.

\section{How the scores compare with TWIST and Agrimate}
\label{sec:compare}

Design differences are in \S\ref{sec:vs-agrimate}. This section is the
scorecard: on objects Agrimate actually published, where do we agree, and
where do we not?

\paragraph{TWIST.}
TWIST is annual, single-commodity, no network, no AMIS~\citep{schewe2017}.
Gate~0 is not a TWIST clone and should not be scored as one. Where TWIST
is the right comparison is storage philosophy: both (and Agrimate) use
operational stock rules rather than a full rational-expectations storage
equilibrium~\citep{deaton1992}. Evaluating Gate~0 first against
Deaton--Laroque would punish a lineage choice. Evaluating it against
TWIST's annual wheat price--supply curve would ignore that we resolved
the network on purpose. The honest TWIST statement is: we inherited the
storage-as-cover idea and discarded the closed-form annual price curve
because Agrimate showed the fortnightly network is first-order for
restriction crises~\citep{headey2011,kuhla2025}.

\paragraph{Agrimate, objects we share.}
Clock, LOWESS harvest, AMIS class map, three-way scenario split, Fig.~3
withheld grain, Fig.~4a-style price panels, Russia--Egypt mechanism, and
Supplement~G rice/maize splits are the shared bar. On those objects:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rice 2008} restriction-led on a non-crash harvest --- aligned.
\item \textbf{Wheat 2010} production-led after detrend --- aligned with
      Agrimate's 2010 reading; our 2008 wheat is more restriction-led than
      their 2007 production-led narrative.
\item \textbf{Maize 2008} is \emph{not} aligned: Agrimate restriction-led,
      SHEAF official split industrial-demand-led. Isolated SHEAF AMIS does
      not carry 2008 maize. That is a model-class difference (RFS residual
      versus their restriction channel), disclosed, not patched.
\item \textbf{Twin / baseline.} Agrimate's unperturbed run still wiggles
      with harvest and storage cost. Ours is an identity at $p_0$. Visual
      comparison of grey baselines is therefore invalid; compare treatment
      deviations.
\item \textbf{Price deflator.} MUV 2010\$ versus CPI. Compare ratios and
      correlation, not levels.
\item \textbf{Stocks.} World MY-end levels versus PSD are tight. Global
      \emph{change} signs are mixed (companion Overleaf
      \texttt{gate0\_agrimate} table: wheat stock-change 4/5 years, maize
      5/5, rice 3/5; supply-change 3/6 all crops). Agrimate's published
      language is stronger than our sign counts.
\item \textbf{28-region maps} --- we cannot replicate; 17+RoW bars only.
\item \textbf{Suppl.~F sensitivity} --- not reproduced.
\item \textbf{2022 wheat} --- we run it; hike matches. Agrimate A.5 is
      wheat-only; we add maize and rice as negative controls.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Agrimate, objects they did not score that we do.}
World consumption levels versus PSD; exporter shipment signs versus
harvest-only and versus PSD volumes; world stocks excluding China. Those
are P1 expansions. They make Gate~0 look \emph{worse} than a price-only
score, which is the point of expanding P1. Consumption trend and some PSD
export levels are not green (\S\ref{sec:leftovers}).

\paragraph{What ``as good as Agrimate'' would require.}
Matching their maize 2008 attribution under \emph{their} scenario split
(no ethanol residual), matching wheat 2007/08 amplitude without overshoot,
matching stock-change language, and a seasonal unperturbed baseline. We
do not currently claim that. We claim: the same checks run; rice 2008 and
wheat 2010 attribution and 2022 wheat hike are in agreement; maize 2008
identification differs for a documented structural reason; leftovers are
listed.

\section{What still misses, on purpose}
\label{sec:leftovers}

These are not a to-do list for the next parameter tweak. They are things
the current spine gets wrong, or only partly right, after we already tried
the obvious patches and rejected them.

\begin{itemize}
\item Wheat 2007/08 still high ($\times 2.27$ versus $\times 1.82$).
\item Wheat 2006/07 residual tightness versus trend (harvest-only still
      $\times 1.46$ versus observed $\sim\times 0.95$ in that window).
\item Exporter commercial stocks on the global STU floor.
\item $\Delta$stock signs at country-year resolution near a coin-flip.
\item Consumption path versus PSD trend (mean flex + isoelastic).
\item AMIS shipment \emph{levels} versus PSD (Russia 2010 $\times 2.39$);
      India rice lingering leak; Ukraine 2010 quota ships up.
\item Maize ex-China MY-end $\times 1.49$ (month mismatch + fat China).
\item Vietnam rice stocks $\times 6$ already in \textbf{2006}, before AMIS.
      India is the AMIS lock on rice stocks.
\item Prescribed FAOSTAT E0 (P2 later).
\item No convenience yield / futures smoothing.
\item Ukraine 2022: AMIS classes only; wartime logistics outside AMIS are
      not in $\tau$. Maize/rice 2022 are weak observed moves.
\end{itemize}

None of these is a license to fit 2008. Substitution is not a patch for
them: it is a cross-crop claim that requires this spine to stay honest.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

Gate~0 is complete enough to write down. With substitution off and
governments not choosing bans, a two-week market with trend-based harvest
shocks, a diary of real export policies, hungry-season buffers, and
exporter selling prices tracks monthly Pink Sheet correlation near $+0.7$
on 2006--11 for all three grains, attributes 2008 wheat and rice to bans
and 2010 wheat to the harvest, matches world stocks at USDA's month,
and---without retuning---matches the 2022 wheat spike as a ban event. It
does not match Agrimate's maize 2008 story, does not track USDA
consumption trends, and does not match several exporter shipment
\emph{volumes}. Those sentences are the result. The next paper turns
substitution on, on this spine, not instead of it.
