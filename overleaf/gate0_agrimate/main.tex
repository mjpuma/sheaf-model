\begin{abstract}
Agrimate (Kuhla et al., 2025) is a single-commodity, 24-step-per-year agent-based
trade model. Its published validation is (i)~wheat world prices and annual
supply/stock changes in the main text (Figs.~3--4), (ii)~a Russia--Egypt
mechanism case (Figs.~5--7), and (iii)~the same price split for rice, maize, and
soybean in Supplement~G. SHEAF's Gate~0 spine is the same clock and the same
forcing (USDA PSD harvests + AMIS quantity cuts), one crop at a time, with
cross-grain substitution and the endogenous export game switched off.
This note reruns Agrimate's \emph{scenario split} on SHEAF and reports, without
cosmetics, where the checks match and where they do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{What Agrimate actually checked}

Agrimate does \emph{not} publish a multi-commodity model. Wheat is the main-text
bar; rice, maize, and soybean are calibrated separately and shown only as
harvest / restricted-exports / world-price panels (Suppl.\ Figs.~G.1--G.3).
The scenarios are always:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{baseline} --- climatology harvest, no export restrictions;
  \item \textbf{production anomalies} --- year-by-year harvest, no restrictions;
  \item \textbf{full} --- harvest anomalies plus historical AMIS restrictions.
\end{enumerate}
World price is the export-volume-weighted offer price, compared to a US-CPI
deflated Pink Sheet series. Annual supply and stock \emph{changes} are compared
to FAOSTAT as percent of 2007--2009 mean supply. Regional maps are wheat, 2007
only.

SHEAF Gate~0 uses the same three legs, plus a fourth \textbf{SHEAF full} that
also forces year-by-year PSD food/feed use and (maize) US industrial/ethanol
excess. Agrimate consumption is endogenous; that extra SHEAF leg is therefore
not in their figures and is plotted only when it moves the series.

Soybean is out of SHEAF's grain set. The 2022 Ukraine wheat exercise
(Agrimate Suppl.\ Fig.~A.5) is not run here.

\section{Where we are not yet as robust as Agrimate}

Running Agrimate's own three legs (not our extra demand block) is the honest test.
It does \emph{not} currently prove we model as well as they do.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Wheat matched full} gets a 2007/08 hike ($\times 1.45$ vs obs $\times 1.82$)
        but also a spurious 2006/07 spike and a 2010 hike of $\times 0.71$ (price
        \emph{falls}). Year-by-year demand is what repairs 2010 ($\times 1.04$).
        Agrimate's Fig.~4a does not need that extra forcing.
  \item \textbf{Maize matched full} (harvest + AMIS, no ethanol) has corr $-0.07$
        and a 2008 hike of only $\times 1.20$. Agrimate Suppl.\ Fig.~G.2 shows a
        restriction-led 2008 peak on a stable harvest. We do \emph{not} reproduce
        that with their scenario split. The ethanol block recovers 2008
        ($\times 1.75$ vs $\times 1.84$) and then overshoots 2010--11.
  \item \textbf{Rice matched full} is the closest Agrimate analogue: corr $+0.62$,
        2010 hike $\times 0.96$ vs obs $\times 0.79$. Forcing year-by-year demand
        \emph{worsens} rice (corr $+0.37$, 2010 $\times 1.90$).
  \item \textbf{Lean-season spike.} SHEAF baseline is flat at $p_0$; Agrimate's
        unperturbed run still has harvest/storage seasonality. Our crisis paths
        still spike every spring vs a nearly flat Pink Sheet.
  \item \textbf{Global stock-change signs} are 3/5 years --- not Agrimate's
        ``sign corresponds except 2008 and 2011 supplies.''
  \item \textbf{Egypt consumption} is a square wave to near-zero some months
        (pipeline warehouse), not Agrimate's smooth purchaser storage.
  \item \textbf{Russia share of Egypt receipts} \emph{does} drop in 2010/11 under
        AMIS --- that mechanism check passes.
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity (their Suppl.~F)} not reproduced.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Verdict.} We are running the same \emph{checks}. We are not yet matching
Agrimate's published maize or wheat visual hindcasts under their scenario split.
Maize 2008 in SHEAF is an ethanol story; in Agrimate it is a restriction story.
Substitution stays blocked.
