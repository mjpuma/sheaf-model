# A2b — price flexibility of the assembled map

Uniform harvest shortfall applied to every country and every step of
the climatological path, with the twin held at its unshocked values
(so the shortfall registers as scarcity). No AMIS, no demand shifter,
no industrial excess. Flexibility is dlog p / dlog H measured on the
mean price over the last four years of the window, after the
shortfall has propagated into stocks.

| crop | eta | omega | rho | 1/|eps| (flow ref) | flexibility at -1% | -2% | -5% | -10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wheat | 1.00 | 0.70 | 0.65 | 6.7 | 6.91 | 0.95 | -2.68 | -4.02 |
| maize | 0.85 | 0.80 | 0.65 | 4.0 | 22.51 | 9.77 | 1.97 | -0.72 |
| rice | 0.95 | 0.72 | 0.65 | 5.0 | -4.52 | -4.80 | -5.12 | -5.20 |

## What this says

The scarcity term is inverse demand over a stock with elasticity
-1/eta. The assembled map's flexibility is much smaller than the flow
reference 1/|eps|, for three compounding reasons, all of them
documented rather than hidden: omega puts most of the weight on the
mean offer price rather than on the marginal valuation, rho smooths,
and the shortfall is measured against a stock (order 100 MMT) rather
than against a per-period consumption flow (order 25 MMT/step).

The consequence worth stating to a referee: eta is not an elasticity
of demand in the sense that eps is, and the two are not required to
agree. What the model does claim is a monotone, symmetric price
response to accessible stock. The flexibility table is the honest
summary of how strong that response is.
